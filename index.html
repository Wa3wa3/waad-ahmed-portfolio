Case Study

Dental Clinic Management MVP

The ask

A stakeholder wanted "dental clinic management software" - shared patient data, appointment scheduling, reminders, and help with clinical decision-making. No further detail. That's a brief wide enough to become a year-long build if left unscoped.

Scoping decisions

  • Confirmed this was a prototype to validate the idea, not a production system - changes everything about how much rigor to build in up front.
  • Chose a browser-based tool for clinic staff over a native app, for fastest iteration.
  • Narrowed "clinical decision-making help" to rule-based alerts (allergy and drug-interaction flags, protocol checklists) instead of AI-generated diagnosis - lower risk, and still demonstrates the core value.
  • Kept it staff-only with automated outbound reminders, rather than building a full patient self-service portal that wasn't asked for.

What shipped

A working web app: shared patient records across logged-in staff, an appointment scheduler, and a live clinical-alert engine - selecting a patient and a procedure together surfaces real-time warnings (for example, a penicillin allergy flagged against a procedure that typically uses amoxicillin) plus a step-by-step protocol checklist for that procedure. A reminders dashboard tracks which upcoming appointments are due a reminder and logs what would be sent.

What's next

Documented explicitly what a production version still needs: real authentication, encryption at rest, a compliance review for health data, audit logging, a real SMS/WhatsApp integration in place of the mock, and clinical sign-off on the alert rules from a licensed dentist before they influence real care.

Case Study

Institutional Wellbeing Program Design

The ask

Design a complete, evidence-based program for institutional employee mental and emotional wellbeing for a large employer, covering structure, methodology, and rollout over two years. The brief was bilingual, dense, and mandated an exact 20-section format with zero tolerance for unsupported claims - a program design that didn't match the structure, or that named a methodology without backing it, was a non-starter.

Approach

  • Built the program section-by-section to the mandated 20-section structure, rather than a generic pitch deck.
  • Grounded every scientific or methodological decision in real, cited sources - WHO's workplace mental health guidelines, the ISO 45003 psychosocial risk standard, published burnout and psychological-safety research, and actual employee-assistance-program ROI studies - instead of vague "evidence-based" language with nothing behind it.
  • Clearly separated what could be designed from methodology and public evidence alone from what genuinely required the commissioning organization's own data (team credentials, pricing, past client references) - flagged explicitly rather than invented.

Outcome

A complete, properly structured, defensible wellbeing program design - every required section present, every scientific claim sourced, and a clear checklist of exactly what the organization needed to fill in before adoption.
